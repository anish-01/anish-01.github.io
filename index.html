Current research

An NVIDIA-sponsored study in Dr. Yunhe Feng's Responsible AI Lab, running since September 2026.

What happens to vision-language model performance when the NVIDIA DGX Spark runs hot for a long time?

I'm testing open-weight models from 2B to 72B parameters under sustained load, then lining up every request's speed against what the hardware was doing at that moment.

Time to first tokenper request
Throughputtokens per second
GPU temperature and clocksampled every second
Power and memorysampled every second
My role
Designing the experiment and building the Python measurement harness.
Why it matters
If sustained heat quietly slows inference, published benchmarks overstate what people get in practice.
Methods
Controlled comparisons, paired statistics, and confidence intervals, with reproducible data collection.
What it will produce
An open-source harness that anyone with the hardware can rerun.

Research

Undergraduate Research Assistant, Responsible AI Lab

University of North Texas, Sept. 2026 to present

I'm designing an NVIDIA-sponsored study of how sustained thermal load affects vision-language model inference on the NVIDIA DGX Spark, across open-weight models from 2B to 72B parameters. I'm building a Python harness that lines up per-request latency (time to first token, throughput) with GPU telemetry sampled once a second: temperature, clock speed, power, and memory. The harness will be released as open source.

Where I'm headed

I want to specialize in AI and machine learning, with a focus on making large models reliable and trustworthy. Longer term, I plan to pursue quantum machine learning, where my computer science and physics training meet. Robotics is the third thread: I started with hardware, and I want to build intelligent systems that act in the physical world.
